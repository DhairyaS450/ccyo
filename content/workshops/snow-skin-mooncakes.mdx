    - Toast the dusting flour. Put the extra 30 g of glutinous rice flour in a dry pan over low heat and stir for about 5 minutes until it smells faintly nutty. Tip onto a plate to cool.
    - Whisk the skin batter. In a heatproof bowl, whisk the glutinous rice flour, rice flour, wheat starch and sugar together. Pour in the milk and oil and whisk until completely smooth. Add the colouring if using. Pass through a sieve if you see lumps.
    - Steam. Cover the bowl tightly with foil so no water drips in. Steam over high heat for 20 minutes until the batter has set into a firm, translucent dough.
    - Knead. Stir the hot dough with a spatula, then once it is cool enough to handle, knead with lightly oiled hands until smooth and glossy, about 3 minutes. Cover and chill for 30 minutes.

This is the recipe we use at the [October 1 workshop](/events/snow-skin-mooncakes), written out so you can make a batch at home. Everything scales cleanly, so use the buttons on the recipe card to make six for a small kitchen or twenty-four for the whole family.

Snow skin mooncakes are the friendliest mooncake to make. There is no pastry to blind-bake, no egg wash to get streaky, and no oven at all. The wrapper is steamed, kneaded, chilled and pressed. If you have ever made mochi or tangyuan you already know the texture.

## 冰皮 What makes it snow skin

The skin is a blend of three flours, and each one does a job:

- **Glutinous rice flour** gives the chew. On its own it turns gummy, so we cut it with the other two.
- **Rice flour** keeps the skin tender and stops it turning into a mochi ball.
- **Wheat starch** is what makes the dough translucent and lets it hold a sharp stamp from the mould.

Milk and a little oil make the dough soft and keep it from drying out in the fridge. Sugar sweetens it, but the filling carries most of the sweetness, so the skin stays quite plain.

## 工具 Tools

You need a steamer, a heatproof bowl that fits in it, a kitchen scale, and a mooncake mould. The plunger-style plastic moulds sold as 50 g with interchangeable plates are the easiest to use and cost a few dollars online. Without a mould you can still make these, just roll them into smooth balls and press a pattern in with the back of a fork or a cookie stamp.

<Callout title="Scale, do not guess">
Snow skin is forgiving, but the skin to filling ratio matters. A 50 g mould wants 25 g of skin and 25 g of filling. Too much skin and the pattern goes soft, too little and the filling peeks through.
</Callout>

## 技巧 Tips

- The dough is sticky when it comes out of the steamer. Do not add more flour; oil your hands and keep kneading. It smooths out as it cools.
- Toasted flour is your friend. Raw glutinous rice flour tastes chalky, toasting it fixes that and makes it dust better.
- Chill the wrapped balls for ten minutes before pressing if the room is warm. A cold ball takes a sharper stamp.
- Press on a board, not in the air. You want firm, even pressure straight down.

## 变化 Variations

Swap the pink colouring for a teaspoon of matcha in the batter for a green skin, or a tablespoon of cocoa. Purple sweet potato paste and custard both make excellent fillings, and a square of dark chocolate hidden in the centre of a red bean ball is a very good idea.

## 问题 Questions

<Q q="Can I make the dough ahead?">
Yes. The steamed dough keeps for two days in the fridge wrapped tightly. Let it sit out for twenty minutes before wrapping so it is pliable.
</Q>

<Q q="Where do I buy the flours?">
Any Chinese or pan-Asian grocery carries all three. Glutinous rice flour and rice flour are often side by side in similar bags, so read the label twice.
</Q>

<Q q="Do they freeze?">
They freeze well for a month in a sealed container. Thaw in the fridge, not on the counter, so the skin stays smooth.
</Q>
