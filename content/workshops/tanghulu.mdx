Tanghulu is the sound of a night market: a rack of glossy red skewers, sugar cracking between your teeth, sour fruit underneath. It is also the president's favourite dessert, which is how it ended up as the second recipe on this site.

The classic version uses hawthorn berries (山楂), which are tart enough to fight the sugar. Strawberries and grapes are the easy substitute and they photograph better. Mandarin segments and blueberries work too, as long as the fruit is bone dry.

## 糖 The sugar

The whole recipe is one technique: cook sugar syrup to the hard crack stage, dip, cool. Hard crack is 150 to 160 C. Below that the shell stays sticky and pulls at your teeth. Above that it starts to brown and taste bitter. A cheap candy thermometer removes the guesswork, but the ice water test has worked for a few hundred years.

<Callout title="Hot sugar">
Syrup at 160 C burns badly and sticks to skin. Keep the bowl of ice water next to the pan the whole time, dip skewers by the handle, and never touch the syrup to check it. If you are doing this at a workshop, one person dips and everyone else waits behind the table.
</Callout>

## 技巧 Tips

- Do not stir the syrup once it boils. Stirring makes the sugar crystallize and the shell turns cloudy and grainy.
- Tilt the pan so the syrup pools deep at one side. Rolling a skewer through a deep pool gives an even coat in one pass.
- Humidity is the enemy of the finished shell. On a rainy day, eat them straight away.
- If the syrup thickens before you finish dipping, put the pan back over low heat for thirty seconds.

## 问题 Questions

<Q q="Can I use brown sugar or honey?">
Not for the shell. Both contain water and other sugars that keep the coating soft. Plain white sugar sets glass clear.
</Q>

<Q q="Why did my coating turn cloudy?">
Either the syrup was stirred after boiling or a sugar crystal from the side of the pan fell in. Wash down the sides of the pan with a wet brush early on, then leave it alone.
</Q>
