Snow skin mooncakes are the no-bake, chewy, chilled cousins of the classic golden mooncake. On Thursday, October 1 we are making them from scratch at lunch in Room 2700, and while the mooncakes chill, the exec will retell the legend of Chang'e, the woman who lives on the moon.

This page walks through what a snow skin mooncake actually is, what you will make, how the lunch runs, and what to bring. The event card at the bottom has everything in one place, with a button that drops it into your calendar.

## 冰皮 What is a snow skin mooncake?

Traditional mooncakes (月饼) are baked: a thin golden pastry around a dense filling like lotus seed paste, red bean, or five-nut. Snow skin mooncakes (冰皮月饼) skip the oven entirely. The wrapper is a soft, slightly chewy dough made from glutinous rice flour, rice flour and wheat starch, cooked with milk and sugar, wrapped around the filling and pressed in a mould. They are served cold, straight from the fridge, and the skin stays snowy white or pastel.

They came out of Hong Kong bakeries in the late 1980s and spread fast because they are lighter, prettier, and honestly more fun to make than the baked kind. No oven, no egg wash, no waiting for pastry to set. Mix, wrap, press, chill.

<Callout title="Why we picked this">
It is the perfect first workshop. Everything can be prepped ahead, the pressing part is satisfying, and you leave with something you made in the shape of a flower. It also lands a few days after Mid-Autumn Festival (中秋节), which falls on September 25 this year.
</Callout>

## 制作 What you will make

Each person makes two or three mooncakes. Waiting for you at the tables:

- **Snow skin dough**, cooked and rested, in white and pink.
- **Red bean paste** filling, rolled into balls and ready to wrap.
- **Toasted glutinous rice flour** for dusting, so nothing sticks to anything.
- **50 g moulds** with a few stamp plates: a flower, a rabbit, and the character for moon.

You do the wrapping and the pressing. That is the whole craft, and it takes about ten minutes once you have watched it done once.

## 步骤 How the lunch runs

1. **Sign in and wash up.** Grab a spot at a table. There is a station for every four people.
2. **Watch one demo.** Someone from the exec wraps and presses a mooncake start to finish. Then it is your turn.
3. **Wrap.** Flatten a piece of dough into a disc, set the filling in the centre, and gather the edges up around it like a dumpling. Pinch closed, roll smooth.
4. **Dust and press.** Roll the ball in a little toasted flour, drop it into the mould, press firmly on the table, lift, release. Flower.
5. **Chill and listen.** Mooncakes go into a box in the fridge to firm up. While they chill, we tell the story of Chang'e and the moon.
6. **Eat one, take one.** They are best cold. Bring a container if you want the second one for later.

## 嫦娥 The legend of Chang'e

Long ago, ten suns rose together and scorched the earth. The archer Hou Yi shot down nine of them and saved the world. As a reward he was given an elixir of immortality, but he did not want to live forever without his wife, Chang'e, so he gave it to her to keep safe.

One day while Hou Yi was out hunting, his apprentice tried to steal the elixir. Rather than let him have it, Chang'e drank it herself. She became weightless and floated up past the clouds until she landed on the moon, the closest place to earth she could reach. Hou Yi came home, found her gone, and looked up. That night the moon was rounder and brighter than it had ever been.

He set out her favourite fruits and cakes under the moon, and people have done the same on the fifteenth night of the eighth lunar month ever since. Look closely at the full moon and you can see Chang'e, and the jade rabbit that keeps her company.

> The moon is the same moon everywhere. That is the whole point of the festival: wherever your family is, you are looking at the same thing tonight.

## 问题 Questions

<Q q="Do I need to know how to cook?">
No. If you can roll play-dough into a ball you are qualified. We show you once and then help as you go.
</Q>

<Q q="Anything with nuts or gluten?">
The skin is made from rice flours and wheat starch, and the filling is red bean. There are no nuts in the recipe. Wheat starch comes from wheat, so treat it as containing gluten. If you have an allergy, ask us on the day and we will check the labels with you.
</Q>

<Q q="Is it free?">
Yes. CCYO covers the ingredients.
</Q>

</Q>
